\begin{abstract}
A weak predictive feature can remain poorly learned because its isolated learning is inherently slow, a stronger cue suppresses cross-entropy sample weights, or coupled parameter geometry actively penalizes its update. These explanations are not causally equivalent. We introduce a matched weak-only counterfactual: the paired models share initialization, data sequence, weak coordinates, and noise; only the strong cue is ablated. Causal starvation requires both outcome suppression and a nondegenerate weak-only first hit; gate failure leaves causal status indeterminate. For full-batch logistic gradient flow, we derive exact finite-width dynamics, $\dot M_w=c_w^\top q(r)/n$ and $\dot r=\Theta q(r)/n$, and the pathwise identity $\Delta=P-N$. In a frozen matched CIFAR-10 study, all 8/8 fresh seed blocks pass the causal certificate; the cue-trained model loses 19.9 percentage points on neutral inputs and 31.9 points under cue conflict. A common fresh linear head recovers part, but not all, of the neutral gap. Controlled recurrent studies further show that starvation is regime-dependent. Finally, Counterfactual Drift Correction (CDC) provides a unique minimum-norm instantaneous intervention in a fixed parameter chart, but operates as a local mechanism control rather than a globally superior optimizer.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
When a deep neural network learns one dominant feature while neglecting another predictive cue, the neglected signal is frequently described as ``starved'' \citep{pezeshki2021gradient}. However, observing a small response to a weak feature during training is not by itself causal evidence of gradient starvation: the weak feature may simply be unlearnable under the chosen architecture, optimization horizon, or signal-to-noise ratio. The distinction is acute in recurrent neural networks, where cue strength, temporal separation, parameter sharing, and non-static geometry jointly govern representation learning \citep{bengio1994learning,hochreiter1997lstm,cho2014gru}.

To isolate the causal effect of a strong cue, we introduce a matched counterfactual intervention grounded in formal causal inference \citep{pearl2009causality,peters2017elements}. A both-feature model $\mathcal{M}_{\Bcond}$ is paired with a weak-only model $\mathcal{M}_{\Wcond}$ that shares identical weight initialization, sample sequence, weak inputs, and optimizer state; only the strong input channel is zeroed out. Tracking the paired weak-feature response gap $\Delta(\tau) = M_w^{\Bcond}(\tau) - M_w^{\Wcond}(\tau)$ and its time derivative $d(\tau) = \dot{\Delta}(\tau)$, positive $d$ indicates \emph{relative transfer}, negative $d$ indicates \emph{rate suppression}, and negative $\Delta$ indicates \emph{outcome suppression}. We reserve the term \emph{causal starvation} strictly for settings where outcome suppression occurs alongside a successful weak-only first hit of a prespecified target $\beta$ within horizon $H$. When the weak-only gate fails, causal status is indeterminate: neither starvation nor its absence is established.

\paragraph{Four core contributions.}
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*]
  \item \textbf{Matched causal diagnosis.} We formalize the matched weak-only counterfactual and establish a two-condition causal certificate, proving that weak learning failure alone is insufficient to diagnose starvation.
  \item \textbf{Exact finite-width dynamics.} We derive exact response--logit flow equations for full-batch logistic gradient flow, $\dot M_w = \frac1n c_w^\top q(r)$ and $\dot r = \frac1n \Theta q(r)$, separating cross-entropy weighting from parameter geometry. The pathwise identity $\Delta(t) = P(t) - N(t)$ establishes that rate suppression yields outcome suppression if and only if accumulated suppression exceeds accumulated transfer.
  \item \textbf{Regime-dependent empirical evidence.} A frozen one-architecture CIFAR-10 confirmation yields causal certificates in 8/8 fresh paired seeds, persistent core-response deficits, and large neutral/conflict accuracy harm. In the audited recurrent results, tanh passes the at-hit certificate in 8/8 seeds (3/8 transfer then suppress; 5/8 suppress throughout), whereas GRU remains indeterminate because its weak-only gate fails in 8/8 seeds.
  \item \textbf{Prediction limits and local mechanism control.} We characterize the scope of frozen-tangent models and prove that Counterfactual Drift Correction (CDC) is the unique minimum-norm instantaneous target-attaining intervention in a fixed parameter chart, providing localized mechanism control without asserting global optimizer superiority.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Related work}
\paragraph{Feature starvation and shortcut learning.}
Cross-entropy loss can favor rapidly learnable features strongly enough to suppress gradient signal to slower predictive features, an effect termed gradient starvation \citep{pezeshki2021gradient}. Shortcut learning, simplicity bias, and early memorization describe the broader empirical tendency of deep networks to exploit low-complexity, unintended cues at the expense of robust core features \citep{arpit2017memorization,geirhos2020shortcut,shah2020pitfalls,herrmann2020shapes,scimeca2022which}. Invariant risk minimization and failure-guided debiasing seek representations invariant to spurious correlations \citep{arjovsky2019irm,nam2020lff}. Our contribution is diagnostic: the matched weak-only counterfactual explicitly tests whether the neglected feature would have been learned in the absence of the strong cue, while direct drift and pathwise attribution distinguish transient rate suppression from adverse outcome suppression.

Methods such as Group DRO, Just Train Twice, and last-layer retraining target worst-group performance under spurious correlations \citep{sagawa2020groupdro,liu2021jtt,kirichenko2023dfr}. We address a distinct question---causal attribution of feature-level learning dynamics---and do not propose a competing robustness benchmark. Our semi-real study evaluates controlled cue ablation on standard image benchmarks \citep{lecun1998mnist,xiao2017fashion}, preserving real core pixels while isolating causal feature interactions.

\paragraph{Implicit bias and margin dynamics.}
On separable classification problems trained with exponential or logistic losses, gradient flow exhibits an implicit bias toward directional maximum-margin separators in linear and homogeneous models \citep{soudry2018implicit,gunasekar2018implicit,ji2019implicit}. While asymptotic margin maximization governs directional convergence at infinite time, gradient starvation is an optimization-time transient phenomenon where rapid growth of dominant logit responses diminishes sample weights before secondary features can develop. Our exact finite-width equations capture this trajectory-level competition directly.

\paragraph{Wide and recurrent learning dynamics.}
Neural tangent kernels (NTK) characterize infinite-width gradient flow in the lazy training regime \citep{jacot2018ntk,du2019gradient,arora2019exact,chizat2019lazy}, and wide-network linearization motivates frozen-tangent approximations \citep{lee2019wide}. Beyond the lazy regime, feature learning limits describe representation movement in overparameterized models \citep{yang2021tensor,bordelon2022learning}. Our response--logit equations are instead exact at finite width for arbitrary trajectories. Freezing their kernels defines a nonlinear logistic tangent surrogate, but transferring its predictions to a trained network requires kernel-movement bounds that we do not establish for tanh RNNs or GRUs. Low-dimensional overlap theories for low-rank RNNs demonstrate that loss-invisible modes retain training history \citep{ger2026invisible}, echoing classical insights on disordered recurrent dynamics \citep{sompolinsky1988chaos} and cautioning against closure in only the visible response coordinates. Mean-field descriptions of structure and disorder in trained recurrent circuits \citep{clark2026structure} study a distinct long-time equilibrium setting. We therefore do not claim a general recurrent optimization-time mean-field theory.

\paragraph{Gradient surgery and multi-task optimization.}
Multi-task learning often suffers from destructive gradient interference, inspiring multi-objective formulations including MGDA \citep{sener2018mgda}, conflict-averse descent (CAGrad) \citep{liu2021cagrad}, gradient sign dropout (GradDrop) \citep{chen2020graddrop}, rotational alignment (Rotograd) \citep{javaloy2022rotograd}, and bargaining game approaches \citep{navon2022nash}. PCGrad projects conflicting task gradients onto orthogonal subspaces \citep{yu2020pcgrad}; Bloop stabilizes auxiliary projections using an exponential moving average \citep{hsieh2024bloop}. CDC also employs a projection, but its protected direction is the gradient of a strong-feature response and its target is derived from the matched weak-only trajectory. That extra counterfactual requires a constructible ablation, a second model, lockstep training, and full-batch gradients. Our comparisons use adapted shadow-target variants and support neither equivalence to the original methods nor broad CDC superiority.

\section{Matched causal diagnosis}
\subsection{The matched weak-only counterfactual}
Let condition $C \in \{\Bcond, \Wcond\}$ denote the both-feature and weak-only training runs, respectively. The two models share an identical parameter initialization $\theta_{\Bcond}(0) = \theta_{\Wcond}(0) = \theta_0 \in \R^P$, identical training sequence samples $(x_i, y_i)_{i=1}^n$, and identical optimization hyperparameters. The only difference is an intervention on the input: in $\Wcond$, the strong cue channel is set identically to zero ($x_{s,i} \equiv 0$).

Let $M_s^C(\theta), M_w^C(\theta)$ be differentiable scalar functionals measuring the model's response to isolated strong and weak cues. For linear models, these correspond to channel-localized mode projections; for nonlinear recurrent models, they are defined via odd symmetric unit-probe contrasts: $M_a^C(\theta) = [f_C(x_a^+; \theta) - f_C(x_a^-; \theta)] / 2$.
For logistic gradient flow on full-batch signed logits $r_i^C(\theta) = y_i f_C(x_i; \theta)$, the empirical loss is:
\begin{equation}
  L_C(\theta) = \frac{1}{n} \sum_{i=1}^n \log\left(1 + e^{-r_i^C(\theta)}\right), \qquad \dot{\theta}_C = -\nabla L_C(\theta_C).
  \label{eq:logistic-flow}
\end{equation}
The paired response gap $\Delta(\tau)$ and instantaneous equal-time relative drift $d(\tau)$ are:
\begin{equation}
  \Delta(\tau) = M_w^{\Bcond}(\tau) - M_w^{\Wcond}(\tau), \qquad d(\tau) = \dot{\Delta}(\tau) = \dot{M}_w^{\Bcond}(\tau) - \dot{M}_w^{\Wcond}(\tau).
  \label{eq:paired-gap}
\end{equation}
Since $\theta_{\Bcond}(0) = \theta_{\Wcond}(0)$, the gap begins at zero: $\Delta(0) = 0$.

\subsection{Formal causal-starvation certificate}
We now state the central definition of the paper.

\begin{definition}[Causal starvation certificate]
\label{def:causal-starvation}
Fix a target response threshold $\beta > 0$ and optimization horizon $H > 0$. Let $\tau_W^*(\beta) = \inf\{\tau \in [0, H] : M_w^{\Wcond}(\tau) \ge \beta\}$ denote the first hitting time of the weak-only control, with $\inf \varnothing = \infty$.
A training run exhibits a \textbf{causal starvation certificate} if and only if:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*]
  \item \textbf{Nondegenerate weak-only learnability:} $\tau_W^*(\beta) \in (0, H]$ with $\beta > M_w^{\Wcond}(0)$; and
  \item \textbf{Outcome suppression at target:} $\Delta(\tau_W^*(\beta)) < 0$.
\end{enumerate}
If $\tau_W^*(\beta) > H$, the weak-only gate fails and the causal status is \textbf{indeterminate}. If $\beta \le M_w^{\Wcond}(0)$, the target is met at initialization (degenerate) and licenses no causal claim.
\end{definition}

\section{Exact dynamics and pathwise attribution}
\subsection{Exact finite-width response--logit dynamics}
We establish that the coupling between sample weighting and parameter geometry is an exact finite-width chain-rule identity rather than an asymptotic mean-field closure.

\begin{proposition}[Exact finite-width response--logit dynamics]
\label{prop:exact-dynamics}
Under full-batch logistic gradient flow (Eq.~\ref{eq:logistic-flow}) at any finite width and parameter dimension $P$, let $J^C(\tau) \in \R^{n \times P}$ have rows $\nabla r_i^C(\theta_C(\tau))^\top$. Define the empirical tangent kernel $\Theta^C(\tau) = J^C(\tau) J^C(\tau)^\top \in \R^{n \times n}$, response-coupling vector $c_w^C(\tau) = J^C(\tau) \nabla M_w^C(\theta_C(\tau)) \in \R^n$, and sigmoid sample-weight vector $q(r^C)_i = \sigm(-r_i^C)$. Then:
\begin{equation}
  \dot{M}_w^C(\tau) = \frac{1}{n} c_w^C(\tau)^\top q(r^C(\tau)), \qquad \dot{r}^C(\tau) = \frac{1}{n} \Theta^C(\tau) q(r^C(\tau)).
  \label{eq:exact-flow-prop}
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}[Proof Sketch]
By chain rule, $\dot{\theta}_C = -\nabla L_C = \frac{1}{n} \sum_i \sigma(-r_i^C) \nabla r_i^C = \frac{1}{n} (J^C)^\top q(r^C)$. Differentiating $M_w^C$ and $r_i^C$ along $\dot{\theta}_C$ immediately gives Eq.~\eqref{eq:exact-flow-prop}. Full proof in Appendix~\ref{app:proof-prop-exact}.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Interpretation.}
Eq.~\eqref{eq:exact-flow-prop} factorizes learning velocity into two distinct terms:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
  \item $q(r)$: The cross-entropy weighting vector. As logits $r_i$ grow, $q_i \to 0$, diminishing the effective learning rate.
  \item $c_w$: The response geometry vector, reflecting alignment between gradients of $M_w$ and sample logits $r_i$.
\end{itemize}

\begin{corollary}[Closed projected flow under exact realization]
\label{cor:projected-flow}
If signed sample logits admit the exact parameter-independent decomposition $r_i^C = \sum_b z_{ib}^C M_b^C + r_i^\perp$, then:
\begin{equation}
  \dot{M}_a^C = \sum_b G_{ab}^C g_b^C + R_a^C, \qquad G_{ab}^C = \ip{\nabla M_a^C}{\nabla M_b^C}, \qquad g_b^C = \frac{1}{n} \sum_i z_{ib}^C \sigm(-r_i^C),
  \label{eq:projected-dynamics}
\end{equation}
where $R_a^C = \frac{1}{n} \sum_i \sigm(-r_i^C) \ip{\nabla M_a^C}{\nabla r_i^\perp}$. When realization is exact ($r^\perp \equiv 0$), $R_a^C \equiv 0$ and the response dynamics close autonomously.
\end{corollary}

\subsection{Cumulative drift balance}
\begin{proposition}[Cumulative drift balance]
\label{prop:cumulative-drift}
Let $\Delta(\tau)$ be absolutely continuous on $[0, H]$ with $\Delta(0) = 0$ and $d(\tau) = \dot{\Delta}(\tau)$ almost everywhere. Define positive and negative drift components:
\begin{equation}
  P(t) = \int_0^t [d(u)]_+ \dd u, \qquad N(t) = \int_0^t [-d(u)]_+ \dd u.
  \label{eq:pn-def}
\end{equation}
Then for all $t \in [0, H]$:
\begin{equation}
  \Delta(t) = P(t) - N(t), \qquad \text{and} \qquad \Delta(t) < 0 \iff N(t) > P(t).
  \label{eq:pathwise-identity}
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Since $\Delta(t) = \Delta(0) + \int_0^t d(u) \dd u = \int_0^t ([d(u)]_+ - [-d(u)]_+) \dd u = P(t) - N(t)$. Full details in Appendix~\ref{app:proof-prop-drift}.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Core implication.}
A negative relative rate ($d(t) < 0$) does \emph{not} imply outcome suppression ($\Delta(t) < 0$) unless the accumulated negative drift $N(t)$ repays all accumulated positive transfer $P(t)$.

\begin{corollary}[Single-crossing tail-area criterion]
\label{cor:single-crossing}
Suppose there exists $\tau_d \in (0, H)$ such that $d > 0$ almost everywhere on $(0, \tau_d)$ and $d < 0$ on $(\tau_d, H)$. Let $A_+ = P(\tau_d) = \Delta(\tau_d) > 0$. Then for $t \ge \tau_d$, $\Delta(t) = A_+ - N(t)$. Strict outcome suppression occurs by horizon $H$ if and only if $N(H) > A_+$.
\end{corollary}

\subsection{Scope of tangent prediction and movement bounds}
Freezing $\Theta$ and $c_w$ at initialization defines a nonlinear logistic tangent model: $\dot{\bar r} = \frac1n \Theta_0 q(\bar r)$, $\dot{\bar M}_w = \frac1n c_{w,0}^\top q(\bar r)$.

\begin{theorem}[Conditional tangent comparison]
\label{thm:tangent-bound}
Suppose on $[0, t]$ that $\|\Theta(u) - \Theta_0\| \le B_\Theta$, $\|c_w(u) - c_{w,0}\| \le B_c$, and $\|q(r)\| \le V$. Let $a = \|\Theta_0\| / (4n)$, $\phi_a(t) = (e^{at}-1)/a$, and $\psi_a(t) = (e^{at}-1-at)/a^2$. Then:
\begin{equation}
  |M_w(t) - \bar M_w(t)| \le \frac{B_c V}{n} t + \frac{\|c_{w,0}\| B_\Theta V}{4n^2} \psi_a(t).
  \label{eq:tangent-bound}
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}
Theorem~\ref{thm:tangent-bound} is strictly conditional: establishing non-vacuous bounds $B_\Theta, B_c$ for nonlinear recurrent networks (tanh/GRU) remains an open challenge. Thus, tangent predictions operate as empirical event heuristics rather than calibrated trajectory guarantees.

\section{Prediction and Counterfactual Drift Correction}
\subsection{Realized-initialization rate-gap criterion}
For the noiseless rank-one dense linear RNN ($h_{k+1} = Wh_k + Bx_k$, $f(x) = c^\top h_T$), we compute the exact equal-time initial gap.

\begin{theorem}[Realized-initialization rate-gap criterion]
\label{thm:initial-gap}
Let the strong cue have strength $\rho > 0$ at lag 0, and the weak cue have lag $\ell \ge 0$. Define $K = G_{ws}(0) = b_s^\top W^\ell b_w$, $A = G_{ww}(0) = \|W^\ell b_w\|^2 + \|(W^\ell)^\top c\|^2 + \|S_w\|_F^2$, $s_{\Bcond} = \sigm(-[\rho M_s(0) + M_w(0)])$, and $s_{\Wcond} = \sigm(-M_w(0))$. The initial rate gap is:
\begin{equation}
  d_0 = s_{\Bcond}(\rho K + A) - s_{\Wcond} A, \qquad d_0 > 0 \iff K > \frac{A}{\rho}\left(\frac{s_{\Wcond}}{s_{\Bcond}} - 1\right).
  \label{eq:initial-gap-formula}
\end{equation}
Since $\Delta(0) = 0$, $d_0 < 0 \implies \Delta(t) < 0$ for sufficiently small $t > 0$.
\end{theorem}
This criterion is exact at $t=0$ but local: it does not constitute a positive-lag global phase predictor.

\subsection{Counterfactual Drift Correction (CDC)}
To evaluate whether the diagnosed mechanism can be controlled locally, we introduce Counterfactual Drift Correction (CDC). In a fixed Euclidean parameter chart, let $a = \nabla m_s(\theta)$, $p = \nabla m_w(\theta)$, and $v = -\nabla L(\theta)$.
Project $p$ onto the orthogonal complement of $a$:
\begin{equation}
  q = p - \frac{p \cdot a}{\|a\|^2} a \quad (a \ne 0), \qquad \delta = [F_w^{\Wcond} - p \cdot v]_+, \qquad \alpha = \frac{\delta}{\|q\|^2} \quad (q \ne 0).
  \label{eq:cdc-formula}
\end{equation}
The corrected parameter update is $v' = v + \alpha q$.

\begin{theorem}[CDC local guarantee]
\label{thm:cdc-guarantee}
In the fixed parameter chart:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*]
  \item \textbf{Instantaneous preservation:} $a \cdot v' = a \cdot v$ exactly (strong drift is unaffected).
  \item \textbf{Minimum Euclidean norm:} $d^* = \alpha q$ is the unique minimum-norm correction attaining the weak-drift lower bound $p \cdot (v+d) \ge F_w^{\Wcond}$ subject to $a \cdot d = 0$.
  \item \textbf{Local finite-step deviation:} For $L$-Lipschitz $\nabla m_s$, $|m_s(\theta + \eta v') - m_s(\theta + \eta v)| \le \frac{L}{2} \eta^2 (\|v'\|^2 + \|v\|^2) = O(\eta^2)$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}
CDC provides first-order instantaneous mechanism control; it is not a claim of global optimization superiority.

\section{Experimental setup}
\subsection{Controlled linear study}
We evaluate linear RNNs (width 96, spectral gain $g=0.5$, full-batch gradient descent) across a grid of strength ratios $\rho \in \{1, 2, 4, 8\}$ and temporal lags $\ell \in \{0, 2, 4, 8\}$, with 8 paired seeds per cell (seed IDs 4--11). The learnability gate is set to $\beta = 0.25$ with horizon $H = 5.0$. A sign-reversal intervention reverses the strong cue at $(\rho, \text{lag}) = (4, 4)$; it is an equivariance check, not a no-cue negative control.

\subsection{Nonlinear recurrent study}
We evaluate tanh RNNs and GRUs at width 32, gain $g=0.8$, cue noise $\sigma_{\text{noise}} = 0.1$, $(\rho, \text{lag}) = (4, 2)$, and target $\beta = 0.5$ over 8 paired seeds. We evaluate instantaneous drift via direct automatic differentiation ($F_w = \nabla M_w \cdot \dot{\theta}$).

\subsection{Frozen matched CIFAR-10 confirmation}
Before inspecting the cue-present outcomes, we froze one CNN4-GN architecture, eight fresh paired seeds, SGD with momentum, a 4,710-update cosine schedule, and all endpoints. B appends a removable label-correlated ribbon (reliability 0.9, amplitude 0.5) outside the $32\times32$ core; W receives the identical core and a neutral zero-valued ribbon after preprocessing. Within each seed block, B and W share initialization, source-image split, minibatch order, augmentation randomness, and optimizer configuration. The primary response is balanced Brier skill on a fixed neutral diagnostic set, with $\beta=0.5$ and scale $S=0.75$. Final behavior uses neutral, random, consistent, and nine within-image conflict cues. E4-lite freezes each final encoder and fits the same balanced linear-head protocol on common neutral inputs. This discrete minibatch experiment tests the matched causal certificate, not the continuous-time tail-area identity.

\subsection{Tangent and intervention evaluations}
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
  \item \textbf{Held-out tangent event test:} 64 crossed records (4 data seeds $\times$ 8 model seeds) evaluating drift and response crossing classifications.
  \item \textbf{CDC mechanism control:} Comparison of ERM, Spectral Decoupling, CDC, and adapted shadow-target variants (Bloop-style, PCGrad-style) across 256 method runs.
  \item \textbf{Semi-real image study:} Controlled cue intervention on MNIST (3-vs-8) and FashionMNIST (0-vs-6) over 64 crossed records.
\end{itemize}

\section{Limitations and conclusion}
\subsection{Scope and limitations}
The empirical evidence is restricted to controlled synthetic grids and one preregistered CIFAR-10 intervention using an artificial ribbon cue and one architecture. It establishes a controlled existence result and diagnostic utility, not the prevalence of starvation under natural shortcuts or a universal architecture ranking. The CIFAR experiment uses minibatch SGD and therefore does not verify the continuous-time tail-area theorem. The tangent surrogate requires unproved movement bounds to supply calibrated trajectories, and CDC requires an exact matched shadow model under full-batch gradient flow in a fixed parameter chart.

\subsection{Conclusion}
Weak learning failure alone is not gradient starvation. By introducing a matched weak-only counterfactual, deriving exact finite-width dynamics $\dot M_w = \frac1n c_w^\top q(r)$, and establishing cumulative drift balance $\Delta = P - N$, we establish a rigorous causal and dynamical framework for feature starvation. The resulting causal chain---exact dynamics $\to$ matched intervention $\to$ pathwise attribution $\to$ local mechanism control---disciplines the study of feature competition in deep networks.

\subsection{Paired logistic gradient flow}
Let $\mathcal{D}_n = \{(x_i, y_i)\}_{i=1}^n$ be a dataset with binary labels $y_i \in \{-1, +1\}$ and inputs $x_i = (x_{s,i}, x_{w,i}, x_{\text{bg},i}) \in \R^{d_s + d_w + d_{\text{bg}}}$.
For condition $C \in \{\Bcond, \Wcond\}$, the parameter $\theta_C(\tau) \in \R^P$ evolves under full-batch gradient flow:
\begin{equation}
  L_C(\theta) = \frac{1}{n} \sum_{i=1}^n \log\left(1 + e^{-y_i f_C(x_i; \theta)}\right), \qquad \frac{\dd \theta_C}{\dd \tau} = -\nabla L_C(\theta_C),
  \label{eq:app-flow}
\end{equation}
where $f_{\Bcond}(x; \theta) = f(x; \theta)$ and $f_{\Wcond}(x; \theta) = f((0, x_w, x_{\text{bg}}); \theta)$. Both systems share initialization $\theta_{\Bcond}(0) = \theta_{\Wcond}(0) = \theta_0$.

\subsection{Hitting time metrics and weak-AUC deficit}
For any response functional $M(\tau)$ and target threshold $\beta > 0$:
\begin{equation}
  \tau(\beta; H) = \inf\{\tau \in [0, H] : M(\tau) \ge \beta\}, \qquad (\inf \varnothing = \infty).
  \label{eq:app-hitting}
\end{equation}
The paired weak-AUC deficit over horizon $H$ is defined as:
\begin{equation}
  \mathcal{A}_w = \int_0^H \left(M_w^{\Wcond}(\tau) - M_w^{\Bcond}(\tau)\right) \dd \tau = -\int_0^H \Delta(\tau) \dd \tau.
  \label{eq:app-auc}
\end{equation}

\subsection{Closed projected flow under exact realization}
\begin{proposition}[Closed projected flow]
\label{prop:app-projected}
If signed sample logits satisfy $r_i^C(\theta) = \sum_b z_{ib}^C M_b^C(\theta) + r_i^\perp(\theta)$, then:
\begin{equation}
  \dot M_a^C = \sum_b G_{ab}^C g_b^C + R_a^C,
  \label{eq:app-projected-flow}
\end{equation}
where $G_{ab}^C = \ip{\nabla M_a^C}{\nabla M_b^C}$, $g_b^C = \frac1n \sum_i z_{ib}^C \sigm(-r_i^C)$, and $R_a^C = \frac1n \sum_i \sigm(-r_i^C) \ip{\nabla M_a^C}{\nabla r_i^\perp}$.
When realization is exact ($r_i^\perp \equiv 0$), $R_a^C \equiv 0$ and the response dynamics close autonomously.
\end{proposition}

\subsection{Log drift-ratio identity}
\begin{theorem}[Log drift-ratio identity]
\label{thm:app-log-ratio}
For rank-one positive cues $z^{\Bcond} = (\rho, 1)$, $z^{\Wcond} = (0, 1)$, write $F_w^{\Bcond} = s_{\Bcond} H_{\Bcond}$ and $F_w^{\Wcond} = s_{\Wcond} H_{\Wcond}$ where $H_{\Bcond} = \rho G_{ws}^{\Bcond} + G_{ww}^{\Bcond}$ and $H_{\Wcond} = G_{ww}^{\Wcond}$. Define $\Psi = \log(F_w^{\Bcond} / F_w^{\Wcond})$. Then $\operatorname{sign}(d) = \operatorname{sign}(\Psi)$, and:
\begin{equation}
  \dot{\Psi} = -s_{\Bcond}(1-s_{\Bcond}) Q_{\Bcond} + s_{\Wcond}(1-s_{\Wcond}) H_{\Wcond} + \frac{\dd}{\dd \tau} \log\left(\frac{H_{\Bcond}}{H_{\Wcond}}\right),
  \label{eq:app-psi-dot}
\end{equation}
where $Q_{\Bcond} = \rho^2 G_{ss}^{\Bcond} + 2\rho G_{sw}^{\Bcond} + G_{ww}^{\Bcond} \ge 0$.
\end{theorem}

\subsection{Every-width six-scalar solvable zero-lag limit}
\begin{theorem}[Every-width six-scalar closure at zero lag]
\label{thm:app-six-scalar}
At zero temporal lag and zero background noise in the dense linear RNN, the recurrent weight matrix $W$ is loss-invisible ($\nabla_W L = 0$). With $u_{ab} = b_a \cdot b_b$ and $w = c \cdot c$, the dynamics close at every width $N$:
\begin{equation}
  \dot M_a = g_a w + \sum_b g_b u_{ab}, \qquad \dot u_{ab} = g_a M_b + g_b M_a, \qquad \dot w = 2\sum_b g_b M_b.
  \label{eq:app-six-scalar-eqs}
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

\subsection{Proof of Proposition 1}
\label{app:proof-prop-exact}
Under full-batch logistic gradient flow, $\dot{\theta} = -\nabla L(\theta) = \frac1n \sum_{i=1}^n \sigm(-r_i) \nabla r_i(\theta) = \frac1n J(\theta)^\top q(r)$.
By chain rule, for any response $M_w(\theta)$:
$\dot{M}_w = \ip{\nabla M_w}{\dot{\theta}} = \frac1n \ip{\nabla M_w}{J^\top q(r)} = \frac1n (J \nabla M_w)^\top q(r) = \frac1n c_w^\top q(r)$.
Similarly, $\dot{r}_i = \ip{\nabla r_i}{\dot{\theta}} = \frac1n \sum_j \ip{\nabla r_i}{\nabla r_j} q_j = \frac1n (\Theta q(r))_i$. \qed

\subsection{Proof of Proposition 2}
\label{app:proof-prop-drift}
Since $\Delta(0) = 0$ and $\Delta(\tau)$ is absolutely continuous, $\Delta(t) = \int_0^t \dot{\Delta}(u) \dd u = \int_0^t d(u) \dd u$.
Writing $d(u) = [d(u)]_+ - [-d(u)]_+$ gives $\Delta(t) = \int_0^t [d(u)]_+ \dd u - \int_0^t [-d(u)]_+ \dd u = P(t) - N(t)$.
Thus $\Delta(t) < 0 \iff P(t) - N(t) < 0 \iff N(t) > P(t)$. \qed

\subsection{Proof of Theorem 1}
\label{app:proof-thm-tangent}
Let $e_r(t) = r(t) - \bar r(t)$ and $e_M(t) = M(t) - \bar M(t)$. Differentiating:
$\dot e_r = \frac1n [\Theta(t) q(r(t)) - \Theta_0 q(\bar r(t))] = \frac1n [\Theta(t) - \Theta_0] q(r(t)) + \frac1n \Theta_0 [q(r(t)) - q(\bar r(t))]$.
Since $\sigm'$ is bounded by $1/4$, $\|q(r) - q(\bar r)\| \le \frac14 \|e_r\|$.
Applying Grönwall's inequality with $a = \|\Theta_0\| / (4n)$ yields the bounds in Eqs.~\eqref{eq:tangent-bound}. \qed

\subsection{Proof of Theorem 2}
\label{app:proof-thm-initial}
At $\tau=0$, $\theta_{\Bcond}(0) = \theta_{\Wcond}(0)$. Using the dense linear block gradients with $W(0), B(0), c(0)$, $G_{ws}(0) = b_s^\top W^\ell b_w = K$ and $G_{ww}(0) = A$.
Evaluating the initial weak drift in each condition:
$\dot M_w^{\Bcond}(0) = s_{\Bcond}(\rho K + A)$ and $\dot M_w^{\Wcond}(0) = s_{\Wcond} A$.
Subtracting gives $d_0 = s_{\Bcond}(\rho K + A) - s_{\Wcond} A$. Setting $d_0 > 0$ yields Eq.~\eqref{eq:initial-gap-formula}. \qed

\subsection{Proof of Theorem 3}
\label{app:proof-thm-cdc}
For (1): $a \cdot v' = a \cdot (v + \alpha q) = a \cdot v + \alpha (a \cdot q) = a \cdot v$, since $a \cdot q = a \cdot (p - \frac{p \cdot a}{\|a\|^2} a) = 0$.
For (2): The optimization problem is $\min_d \frac12 \|d\|^2$ subject to $a \cdot d = 0$ and $p \cdot (v + d) \ge F_w^{\Wcond}$. Forming the Lagrangian $L(d, \lambda_1, \lambda_2) = \frac12 \|d\|^2 - \lambda_1 (a \cdot d) - \lambda_2 (p \cdot d - \delta)$, stationarity gives $d^* = \lambda_1 a + \lambda_2 p$. Orthogonality $a \cdot d^* = 0$ forces $\lambda_1 = -\lambda_2 \frac{a \cdot p}{\|a\|^2}$, so $d^* = \lambda_2 q$. Enforcing $p \cdot d^* = \delta$ yields $\lambda_2 = \delta / \|q\|^2 = \alpha$, proving uniqueness.
For (3): By Taylor expansion with Lipschitz gradient $\nabla m_s$, $|m_s(\theta + \eta v') - m_s(\theta + \eta v)| \le \eta |a \cdot (v' - v)| + \frac{L}{2} \eta^2 (\|v'\|^2 + \|v\|^2) = \frac{L}{2} \eta^2 (\|v'\|^2 + \|v\|^2)$. \qed
