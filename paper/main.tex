\title{From Rate Suppression to Outcome Starvation:\\
A Finite-Width Causal Theory of Recurrent Learning}
\author{Anonymous authors}
\date{ICLR 2027 submission draft --- 24 August 2026}

\begin{document}

\begin{abstract}
A strong predictive cue can accelerate optimization while preventing a weaker but
independently predictive cue from being learned.  We study this gradient-starvation
question in recurrent binary classifiers using a paired causal intervention: a
both-feature trajectory is compared with a same-initialization trajectory in which
only the strong input channel is ablated.  Our contribution is a finite-width
package rather than a claimed general dynamical mean-field theory (DMFT).  First,
we derive the exact cross-entropy response flow through a response--logit
cross-kernel and identify the additional assumption under which it reduces to a
closed two-mode equation $\dot M=Gg$.  Second, after a transfer-to-suppression
rate crossing, we prove that outcome starvation occurs if and only if the negative
post-crossing drift area exhausts the earlier transfer advantage.  Third, for a
noiseless rank-one cue law, an ordering-invariant log drift ratio gives sufficient
conditions for a unique rate crossing.  We also prove transverse hitting-time
stability and an exact six-scalar, zero-disorder/zero-lag wide limit with an explicit
$9/(N\epsilon^2)$ initialization bound.  Finally, we derive Counterfactual Drift
Correction (CDC), the unique uncapped minimum-norm correction that matches a
weak-only drift target while preserving instantaneous strong-response drift, and
prove a local $O(\eta^2)$ one-step deviation bound.

Controlled dense-linear experiments exhibit positive weak-learning AUC gaps in
32/32 seeds at lags 0, 2, and 4.  Under the final direct-autograd nonlinear
protocol, however, tanh transfer-to-starvation is seed-dependent (3/8 causal
certificates), while GRU has 5/8 response crossings but 0/8 causal certificates
because the weak-only model never passes the learnability gate.  CDC uniquely
preserves instantaneous strong drift, but does not improve weak rescue or final
strong retention over all shadow-based ablations.  These corrected results delimit
the claim: the causal and finite-width theorems are established; a general
positive-disorder/positive-lag optimization-time DMFT, an analytic phase boundary,
and universal nonlinear crossover remain open.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Cross-entropy training can favor a subset of predictive features strongly enough
that other useful features are learned slowly or not at all.  This phenomenon was
formalized as gradient starvation by \citet{pezeshki2021gradient}.  Recurrent
networks add a temporal geometry: cue strength, lag, shared recurrent parameters,
and evolving feature response geometry all affect which signal receives gradient.
The resulting mechanism is easy to overstate.  A weak-feature drift can decline
without changing sign; its relative drift can change sign without the weak response
falling behind; and a response gap is not evidence of causal starvation if the
weak-only counterfactual is itself unable to learn.

We address these distinctions with a paired intervention.  The both-feature and
weak-only models share initialization, labels, samples, weak coordinates, and
noise; only the strong input channel is set to zero in the weak-only condition.  We
track common feature-response functionals in both trajectories.  This setup
separates three statements:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,nosep]
  \item \emph{relative transfer}: the strong cue initially increases the weak
  response rate;
  \item \emph{rate suppression}: the both-feature weak response later grows more
  slowly than its counterfactual;
  \item \emph{causal outcome starvation}: the both-feature weak response falls
  behind and the weak-only counterfactual demonstrably learns.
\end{enumerate}
The distinction is both mathematical and empirical: the final GRU experiment has
response crossings but fails the causal learnability gate in every seed.

\paragraph{Contributions.}
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*]
  \item We prove an exact finite-width response-flow identity for logistic gradient
  flow.  A closed projected equation $\dot M=Gg$ follows only under exact,
  parameter-independent logit realization; nonlinear probe responses carry an
  explicit residual.
  \item We give a necessary-and-sufficient negative-tail-area criterion converting
  a unique rate crossover into an outcome crossing, together with quantitative
  crossing-time bounds.
  \item For noiseless rank-one cues, we derive an ordering-invariant log drift
  ratio and sufficient conditions for a unique rate crossover.  Unlike a
  non-canonical additive geometry/field split, the ratio has a fixed meaning.
  \item We prove stability of transverse first hitting times and an exact
  zero-disorder, zero-lag six-scalar closure with finite-horizon convergence in
  probability and explicit constants.
  \item We prove three local CDC results: exact instantaneous strong-drift
  preservation, unique uncapped minimum-norm optimality when feasible, and a local
  $O(\eta^2)$ one-step strong-response deviation bound.
  \item We provide executable theorem contracts and a claim-audited experiment
  suite.  The corrected nonlinear evidence is reported as seed-dependent and the
  unsuccessful exploratory search is retained as a negative result.
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{Claim boundary.}
We do not claim that recurrent networks universally undergo transfer-to-starvation,
that a general joint CE-RNN optimization-time DMFT has been solved, or that a
crossover time or analytic $\rho_c$ is independently predicted.  The general DMFT
solver remains deliberately unimplemented where its proof obligations are unmet.

\section{Related work}
\paragraph{Gradient starvation.}
\citet{pezeshki2021gradient} identify feature starvation under cross-entropy and
motivate Spectral Decoupling.  Our focus is different: recurrent temporal features,
a paired strong-channel intervention, exact finite-width response dynamics, and the
logical gap between rate suppression and outcome starvation.

\paragraph{Learning dynamics in recurrent networks.}
\citet{ger2026invisible} derive low-dimensional overlap learning dynamics for
low-rank RNNs and show that overlaps invisible to the loss can retain training
history.  This directly cautions against closing a recurrent learning theory using
only visible response coordinates.  \citet{clark2026structure} develop a
mean-field account of structure and disorder in task-trained recurrent circuits in
a long-time Langevin/Gibbs setting.  That equilibrium construction is distinct from
the paired, noiseless optimization-time gradient flow studied here.  Accordingly,
we claim neither the first learning theory for RNNs nor a completed general RNN
DMFT; our novelty lies in the causal pairing and finite-width rate-to-outcome
results.\footnote{The descriptions of the 2026 sources were paraphrased from the
linked public sources. Content was rephrased for compliance with licensing
restrictions.}

\paragraph{Gradient surgery.}
PCGrad projects conflicting task gradients \citep{yu2020pcgrad}; Bloop stabilizes
an auxiliary projection using an exponential moving average
\citep{hsieh2024bloop}.  Projection itself is not our novelty.  CDC protects the
gradient of a theory-defined strong-feature response and takes its target from a
matched causal weak-only shadow.  This stronger information setting must be
reported: CDC requires a constructible ablation, a second model, lockstep training,
and full-batch gradients.

\section{Causal setup and definitions}
\subsection{Paired logistic gradient flow}
For condition $C\in\{\Bcond,\Wcond\}$, let $\theta_C(\tau)\in\R^P$ follow
full-batch gradient flow on signed logits $r_i^C(\theta)=y_i f_C(x_i;\theta)$:
\begin{equation}
  L_C(\theta)=\frac1{n_C}\sum_{i=1}^{n_C}\log(1+e^{-r_i^C(\theta)}),
  \qquad \dot\theta_C=-\nabla L_C(\theta_C).
  \label{eq:gradient-flow}
\end{equation}
The two conditions share initialization and all data except that the strong channel
is zeroed under $\Wcond$.  Let $M_s^C,M_w^C$ be common differentiable strong and
weak response functionals.  Shared initialization gives
\begin{equation}
  \Delta(0)=0,\qquad
  \Delta(\tau)=M_w^{\Bcond}(\tau)-M_w^{\Wcond}(\tau).
  \label{eq:gap}
\end{equation}
Optimization time $\tau$ is distinct from sequence index $k$.

The controlled dense linear RNN is
\begin{equation}
 h_{k+1}=Wh_k+Bx_k,\qquad f(x)=c^\top h_T,
 \label{eq:linear-rnn}
\end{equation}
with $W_{ij}(0)\sim\mathcal N(0,g^2/N)$,
$B_{ia}(0)\sim\mathcal N(0,1/N)$, and
$c_i(0)\sim\mathcal N(0,1/N)$.  The width scaling of $B$ is essential for a
non-divergent projected metric.  We additionally study tanh RNNs and GRUs.  For
nonlinear models the two responses are common odd symmetric unit-probe contrasts,
$[f(x_a^+)-f(x_a^-)]/2$, not assumed additive coordinates of every data logit.

\subsection{Causal labels and metrics}
Let
\begin{equation}
 d(\tau)=\Delta'(\tau)=F_w^{\Bcond}(\tau)-F_w^{\Wcond}(\tau).
 \label{eq:equal-time-drift}
\end{equation}
We call $d>0$ relative transfer and $d<0$ relative rate suppression.  An outcome
crossing occurs when $\Delta$ reaches zero after a positive excursion and then
becomes negative.  We reserve \emph{causal starvation} for outcome suppression
when the weak-only trajectory also reaches a preregistered target $\beta$ within
horizon $H$.  If the weak-only model does not reach $\beta$, the result is
indeterminate rather than starvation.

We report first-hitting-time delay when both hits are observed.  The horizon-wide
weak AUC gap is
\begin{equation}
  \mathcal A_w=\int_0^H
  \left(M_w^{\Wcond}(\tau)-M_w^{\Bcond}(\tau)\right)\dd\tau,
  \label{eq:auc-gap}
\end{equation}
computed by the logged-grid trapezoid rule.  GSI-5 is retained as a margin-weight
diagnostic, not as the definition of starvation.

\section{Exact finite-width response dynamics}
\begin{theorem}[Universal response--logit cross-kernel flow]
\label{thm:cross-kernel}
At every finite width and every optimization time at which $M_a^C$ is
differentiable,
\begin{equation}
 \dot M_a^C=\frac1{n_C}\sum_i K_{ai}^C\sigm(-r_i^C),
 \qquad K_{ai}^C=\ip{\nabla M_a^C}{\nabla r_i^C}.
 \label{eq:cross-kernel}
\end{equation}
No width limit or two-mode realization is required.
\end{theorem}

\begin{corollary}[Closed projected flow under exact realization]
\label{cor:projected}
If the signed logits admit the exact parameter-independent representation
$r_i^C=\sum_b z_{ib}^C M_b^C$, then
\begin{equation}
 \dot M_a^C=\sum_bG_{ab}^C g_b^C,\quad
 G_{ab}^C=\ip{\nabla M_a^C}{\nabla M_b^C},\quad
 g_b^C=\frac1{n_C}\sum_i z_{ib}^C\sigm(-z_i^C\!\cdot M^C).
 \label{eq:Gg}
\end{equation}
If $r_i^C=z_i^C\cdot M^C+r_i^\perp$, the exact equation instead contains
\begin{equation}
 R_a^C=\frac1{n_C}\sum_i\sigm(-r_i^C)
       \ip{\nabla M_a^C}{\nabla r_i^\perp},
 \qquad \dot M_a^C=(G^Cg^C)_a+R_a^C.
 \label{eq:projection-residual}
\end{equation}
\end{corollary}

For channel-localized modes of the dense linear RNN, differentiation across the
disjoint $(c,B,W)$ blocks gives the exact finite-width geometry
\begin{equation}
 G_{ab}=p_a^\top p_b+
 \ind\{\operatorname{ch}(a)=\operatorname{ch}(b)\}q_a^\top q_b+
 \ip{S_a}{S_b}_F,
 \label{eq:dense-geometry}
\end{equation}
where $p_a=W^{\ell_a}b_a$, $q_a=(W^{\ell_a})^\top c$, and
\begin{equation}
 S_a=\sum_{j=0}^{\ell_a-1}(W^j)^\top c
                 (W^{\ell_a-1-j}b_a)^\top.
\end{equation}
The proofs are in Appendix~\ref{app:finite-proofs}.  Equation~\eqref{eq:Gg} is
exact for the zero-background-noise linear synthetic model.  For tanh/GRU probes,
we use Theorem~\ref{thm:cross-kernel}'s direct autograd drift as primary and report
$R$; projected and matched-state decompositions are diagnostics only.

\section{From rate suppression to outcome starvation}
\begin{theorem}[Necessary and sufficient tail-area criterion]
\label{thm:tail-area}
Let $\Delta$ be absolutely continuous on $[0,H]$, with $\Delta(0)=0$.  Suppose
there is $\tau_d\in(0,H)$ such that $d>0$ almost everywhere on $(0,\tau_d)$ and
$d<0$ almost everywhere on $(\tau_d,H)$.  Define
\begin{equation}
 A_+=\int_0^{\tau_d}d(u)\dd u=\Delta(\tau_d)>0,\qquad
 N(t)=\int_{\tau_d}^{t}-d(u)\dd u.
\end{equation}
Then $\Delta(t)=A_+-N(t)$ for $t\ge\tau_d$.  Hence equality is reached by $H$
if and only if $N(H)\ge A_+$, and strict outcome starvation occurs by $H$ if and
only if $N(H)>A_+$.  If $d<0$ on each nontrivial post-crossing interval, the later
equality is unique whenever it exists.
\end{theorem}

This result blocks a common but false inference: a $+\to-$ drift crossing locates a
local maximum of $\Delta$; it does not force $\Delta$ to return to zero.  The
post-crossing negative area must repay the positive area already accumulated.
Causal starvation further requires weak-only learnability.

\begin{corollary}[Response-crossing bounds]
\label{cor:crossing-bounds}
Under Theorem~\ref{thm:tail-area}, if $d(t)\le-\kappa<0$ on $[t_0,H]$ with
$t_0>\tau_d$, then, when the right side lies within the horizon,
\begin{equation}
 \tau_{\mathrm{response}}\le
 t_0+\frac{[A_+-N(t_0)]_+}{\kappa}
 \le t_0+\frac{A_+}{\kappa}.
\end{equation}
If $d(\tau_d)=0$ and $d'(t)\le-\lambda<0$ on
$[\tau_d,\tau_d+r]$, then
$N(t)\ge\lambda(t-\tau_d)^2/2$; when
$A_+\le\lambda r^2/2$,
\begin{equation}
 \tau_{\mathrm{response}}\le\tau_d+\sqrt{2A_+/\lambda}.
\end{equation}
\end{corollary}

\subsection{An ordering-invariant rank-one criterion}
Assume deterministic positive cues $z^{\Bcond}=(\rho,1)$,
$z^{\Wcond}=(0,1)$, $\rho>0$, and exact two-mode realization.  Define
\begin{align}
 u_B&=\rho M_s^{\Bcond}+M_w^{\Bcond},& s_B&=\sigm(-u_B),\\
 u_W&=M_w^{\Wcond},&s_W&=\sigm(-u_W),\\
 H_B&=\rho G_{ws}^{\Bcond}+G_{ww}^{\Bcond},&
 H_W&=G_{ww}^{\Wcond},\\
 Q_B&=\rho^2G_{ss}^{\Bcond}+2\rho G_{sw}^{\Bcond}+G_{ww}^{\Bcond}\ge0.
\end{align}

\begin{theorem}[Log drift-ratio identity]
\label{thm:log-ratio}
The weak drifts factor as $F_w^{\Bcond}=s_BH_B$ and
$F_w^{\Wcond}=s_WH_W$.  Where $H_B,H_W>0$, define
\begin{equation}
 \Psi=\log\frac{F_w^{\Bcond}}{F_w^{\Wcond}}
 =\log\frac{s_B}{s_W}+\log\frac{H_B}{H_W}.
\end{equation}
Then $\operatorname{sign}(d)=\operatorname{sign}(\Psi)$ and
\begin{equation}
 \Psi'=-s_B(1-s_B)Q_B+s_W(1-s_W)H_W
       +\left(\log\frac{H_B}{H_W}\right)'.
 \label{eq:Psi-prime}
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

\begin{corollary}[Unique rate crossing]
\label{cor:unique-rate-crossing}
Suppose on $[t_0,H]$ that $H_B,H_W>0$, $\Psi(t_0)>0$,
$\Psi'(t)\le-\kappa<0$, and $H-t_0\ge\Psi(t_0)/\kappa$.  Then exactly one
rate crossing occurs in $(t_0,H]$ and
\begin{equation}
 \tau_d\le t_0+\Psi(t_0)/\kappa.
\end{equation}
A sufficient primitive condition is
\begin{equation}
 s_B(1-s_B)Q_B\ge a,\quad s_W(1-s_W)H_W\le b,\quad
 (\log(H_B/H_W))'\le c,\quad a-b-c\ge\kappa>0.
\end{equation}
\end{corollary}

The bounds must be established independently to be predictive.  Evaluating
$\Psi$ on a completed trajectory is certification, not prediction.  Noisy cues
generally break the rank-one factorization, and even a rate crossing still requires
Theorem~\ref{thm:tail-area} for an outcome crossing.

\subsection{Hitting-time stability}
\begin{corollary}[Transverse first-hit stability]
\label{cor:hitting}
Let continuous $x_N,x$ on $[0,H]$ satisfy
$\norm{x_N-x}_\infty\le\epsilon_N$.  Let $\tau\in(0,H)$ be the first upward
crossing of $\beta$, separated from earlier times, and suppose
$x'(t)\ge\kappa>0$ on $[\tau-r,\tau+r]$.  For sufficiently small $\epsilon_N$,
\begin{equation}
 |\tau_N-\tau|\le\epsilon_N/\kappa.
\end{equation}
A logged grid of spacing $h_N$ adds $h_N$.  For paired delays,
\begin{equation}
 |\Delta\tau_N-\Delta\tau|
 \le \epsilon_{B,N}/\kappa_B+\epsilon_{W,N}/\kappa_W+2h_N.
\end{equation}
\end{corollary}
This transfers an independently proved uniform trajectory limit; it does not prove
that limit.

\section{A solvable zero-disorder wide limit}
Set recurrent gain $g=0$, lag separation zero, and background noise zero in the
dense linear model.  Write the input columns as $b_s,b_w$.  The recurrent block is
inert, $M_a=c\cdot b_a$.  Let $u_{ab}=b_a\cdot b_b$ and $w=c\cdot c$.

\begin{theorem}[Every-width six-scalar closure]
\label{thm:six-scalar}
At every width,
\begin{align}
 \dot M_a&=g_aw+\sum_b g_bu_{ab},&
 \dot u_{ab}&=g_aM_b+g_bM_a,\\
 \dot w&=2\sum_bg_bM_b,&
 G_{ab}&=u_{ab}+\delta_{ab}w.
 \label{eq:six-scalar}
\end{align}
\end{theorem}

\begin{theorem}[Finite-horizon deterministic wide limit]
\label{thm:wide-limit}
Let all coordinates of $b_s,b_w,c$ be mutually independent
$\mathcal N(0,1/N)$ and
\begin{equation}
 q_N=(M_s,M_w,u_{ss},u_{sw},u_{ww},w),\qquad
 q_0=(0,0,1,0,1,1).
\end{equation}
For every $\epsilon>0$,
\begin{equation}
 \Prob\!\left(\norm{q_N(0)-q_0}_\infty>\epsilon\right)
 \le \min\left\{1,\frac{9}{N\epsilon^2}\right\}.
 \label{eq:init-concentration}
\end{equation}
On every fixed horizon $[0,H]$, let $L_H$ be a vector-field Lipschitz constant on
a common compact tube containing the finite- and infinite-width trajectories.  Then
\begin{equation}
 \sup_{\tau\le H}\norm{q_N(\tau)-q(\tau)}_\infty
 \le e^{L_HH}\norm{q_N(0)-q_0}_\infty,
 \label{eq:gronwall}
\end{equation}
and
\begin{equation}
 \Prob\!\left(\sup_{\tau\le H}\norm{q_N(\tau)-q(\tau)}_\infty>\delta\right)
 \le\min\left\{1,\frac{9e^{2L_HH}}{N\delta^2}\right\}.
 \label{eq:trajectory-concentration}
\end{equation}
Thus $q_N\to q$ uniformly on fixed horizons in probability, with initialization
scale $O_{\Prob}(N^{-1/2})$.
\end{theorem}

This theorem is intentionally singular: it is a genuine large-width result only at
zero disorder and zero lag.  It is not a positive-disorder DMFT.

\section{Counterfactual Drift Correction}
At a both-feature state $\theta$, let
\begin{equation}
 a=\nabla m_s(\theta),\quad p=\nabla m_w(\theta),\quad
 v=-\nabla L(\theta),\quad F_s=a\cdot v,\quad F_w=p\cdot v.
\end{equation}
A same-initialization weak-only shadow supplies $F_w^{\Wcond}$.  Define
\begin{equation}
 q=\begin{cases}
 p-\dfrac{p\cdot a}{\norm a^2}a,&a\ne0,\\
 p,&a=0,
 \end{cases}
 \quad
 \delta=[F_w^{\Wcond}-F_w]_+,
 \quad
 \alpha=\begin{cases}\delta/\norm q^2,&\delta>0,\ q\ne0,\\0,&\delta=0.
 \end{cases}
 \label{eq:cdc}
\end{equation}
The corrected velocity is $v'=v+\alpha q$.  A positive deficit with $q=0$ is
infeasible.  The theorems assume full-batch gradients, plain SGD, zero weight
decay, and no clipping.  A coefficient cap preserves orthogonality but can lose
target attainment and uncapped optimality.

\begin{theorem}[CDC local guarantees]
\label{thm:cdc}
The construction in Eq.~\eqref{eq:cdc} has the following properties.
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*]
  \item \textbf{Instantaneous preservation:} $a\cdot v'=a\cdot v$ exactly,
  including when $\alpha$ is capped.
  \item \textbf{Minimum norm:} for the feasible uncapped problem, $d^*=\alpha q$
  is the unique minimum-norm correction satisfying
  $a\cdot d=0$ and $p\cdot(v+d)\ge F_w^{\Wcond}$.
  \item \textbf{Local finite-step bound:} if $\nabla m_s$ is $L$-Lipschitz on a
  convex neighborhood containing both step segments, then
  \begin{equation}
  |m_s(\theta+\eta v')-m_s(\theta+\eta v)|
  \le\frac L2\eta^2(\norm{v'}^2+\norm v^2).
  \label{eq:cdc-step}
  \end{equation}
  If $\norm v\le V$, $\delta\le D$, and $\norm q\ge q_{\min}>0$, the right side
  is at most
  \begin{equation}
  \frac L2\eta^2\left[V^2+(V+D/q_{\min})^2\right].
  \end{equation}
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}
The result is one-step and local.  It is not a trajectory guarantee and does not
imply final strong-response retention.

\section{Experimental protocol}
\subsection{Synthetic task and pairing}
Sequences have two input channels.  A strong cue of scale $\rho$ and a unit weak
cue appear at controlled sequence positions separated by a lag.  Positive tasks
align the cues with the label; the negative control reverses the strong cue.  Data
and initialization are paired across both-feature and weak-only conditions.
Training is deterministic full-batch cross-entropy with zero weight decay.  Except
for E-NL, the two conditions can be run sequentially because deterministic
full-batch updates preserve the same trajectories; E-NL uses lockstep models so
both exact drifts are available at each logged time.

\section{Discussion and limitations}
\paragraph{What is proved.}
The finite-width cross-kernel identity, exact-realization corollary, dense-linear
geometry, tail-area equivalence, conditional rank-one crossover criterion,
transverse hitting-time bound, singular zero-disorder wide limit, and local CDC
results are mathematical statements with proofs.  Executable contracts reproduce
their algebra and reject scope violations.

\paragraph{What the experiments establish.}
The controlled dense model shows robust positive AUC gaps in the learnable region.
The nonlinear experiment does not establish a universal crossover: tanh is
seed-dependent, and GRU fails causal learnability despite response crossings.  The
exploratory search did not find a 4/4 robust cell.  CDC demonstrates its exact local
constraint but not a superior practical tradeoff.

\paragraph{Open mean-field theory.}
For positive recurrent disorder or positive lag, a joint optimization-time DMFT
would need (i) an effective single-site process and covariance/response kernels,
(ii) all visible and loss-invisible closure variables, (iii) finite-horizon
existence and uniqueness, (iv) joint quenched concentration under shared disorder,
and (v) discretization and hitting-time control.  None is supplied by fitting an
empirical closure reference.  We therefore leave an analytic $\rho_c$ and
independent tanh/GRU $\tau^*$ prediction open.

\paragraph{Data and external validity.}
All citable results are controlled synthetic experiments.  A Waterbirds adapter
exists but has not been run.  Its proposed weak coordinate is an intercept rather
than an identified bird-shape response, and no exact background ablation exists;
we make no Waterbirds claim.  If pursued, the preregistered sensitivity pilot must
precede a full run and oracle/group-agnostic information settings must be separated.

\paragraph{Reproducibility and baselines.}
The final E-NL rerun is content-addressed and compactly archived, but historical
E1, E2, E2-R, exploratory E-NL, and E3 runs retain a disclosed dirty-source
provenance limitation.  The Bloop-style and PCGrad-style baselines are repository
implementations motivated by the cited methods, not yet independently audited
replicas.  CDC also has a stronger supervision and compute setting than ERM,
Spectral Decoupling, and the interaction penalty.

\paragraph{Submission readiness.}
The package is scientifically defensible as a controlled theory paper, but the
seed-dependent nonlinear headline, lack of real-data evidence, historical
dirty-run provenance, and unaudited external baselines limit its present empirical
strength. No theorem or experiment can guarantee an oral acceptance outcome.

\section{Conclusion}
Gradient starvation requires more than a decreasing weak-feature rate.  In a paired
causal comparison, a rate crossover becomes an outcome crossing exactly when its
negative tail area repays the earlier transfer advantage, and a causal label also
requires weak-only learnability.  This distinction turns an ambiguous mechanism
into a theorem and an executable certificate.  The same discipline narrows the
rest of the paper: $\dot M=Gg$ is exact only under explicit realization assumptions;
the rank-one criterion predicts only when its bounds are independently established;
the wide limit is singular; and CDC's exact guarantee is local.  The corrected
nonlinear results are correspondingly modest.  Establishing a general recurrent
optimization-time theory remains an open problem rather than a numerical placeholder.

\section{Proofs for the finite-width theorem package}
\label{app:finite-proofs}

\subsection{Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:cross-kernel} and
Corollary~\ref{cor:projected}}
For $\ell(r)=\log(1+e^{-r})$, $\ell'(r)=-\sigm(-r)$.  Therefore
\begin{equation}
 \dot\theta_C=-\nabla L_C
 =\frac1{n_C}\sum_i\sigm(-r_i^C)\nabla r_i^C.
\end{equation}
The chain rule gives
\begin{equation}
 \dot M_a^C=\ip{\nabla M_a^C}{\dot\theta_C}
 =\frac1{n_C}\sum_i\ip{\nabla M_a^C}{\nabla r_i^C}\sigm(-r_i^C),
\end{equation}
which proves Theorem~\ref{thm:cross-kernel}.  Under exact realization,
$\nabla r_i^C=\sum_bz_{ib}^C\nabla M_b^C$.  Substitution and exchange of finite
sums yield Eq.~\eqref{eq:Gg}.  If an orthogonal residual logit term is present,
substitution of
$\nabla r_i^C=\sum_bz_{ib}^C\nabla M_b^C+\nabla r_i^\perp$ instead yields
Eq.~\eqref{eq:projection-residual}.  \qed

\subsection{Proof of the dense-linear geometry}
For a unit response mode in channel $a$ at lag $\ell_a$, write $b_a$ for the
corresponding input column.  Direct differentiation of
$c^\top W^{\ell_a}b_a$ gives gradient $p_a=W^{\ell_a}b_a$ with respect to $c$,
gradient $q_a=(W^{\ell_a})^\top c$ with respect to column $b_a$, and recurrent
gradient
\begin{equation}
 S_a=\sum_{j=0}^{\ell_a-1}(W^j)^\top c
       (W^{\ell_a-1-j}b_a)^\top.
\end{equation}
The parameter blocks are disjoint, so their gradient inner products add.  Input
columns contribute only when the channels coincide, giving
Eq.~\eqref{eq:dense-geometry}.  \qed

\subsection{Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:tail-area} and
Corollary~\ref{cor:crossing-bounds}}
Absolute continuity and the fundamental theorem of calculus give
\begin{equation}
 \Delta(\tau_d)=\Delta(0)+\int_0^{\tau_d}d=A_+,
 \qquad
 \Delta(t)=\Delta(\tau_d)+\int_{\tau_d}^{t}d=A_+-N(t).
\end{equation}
The sign assumption makes $N$ continuous and strictly increasing on every
nontrivial post-crossing interval.  Equality, strict negativity, and uniqueness
are therefore respectively equivalent to $N(H)\ge A_+$, $N(H)>A_+$, and strict
post-crossing growth of $N$.

If $d\le-\kappa$ after $t_0$, then
$N(t)-N(t_0)\ge\kappa(t-t_0)$; solve for the time needed to cover the remaining
area.  If $d'(t)\le-\lambda$ and $d(\tau_d)=0$, integration gives
$d(t)\le-\lambda(t-\tau_d)$ and then
$N(t)\ge\lambda(t-\tau_d)^2/2$.  Applying the theorem proves both stated bounds.
\qed

\subsection{Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:log-ratio} and
Corollary~\ref{cor:unique-rate-crossing}}
The deterministic cue law gives
$g^{\Bcond}=s_B(\rho,1)$ and $g^{\Wcond}=s_W(0,1)$.  Equation~\eqref{eq:Gg}
therefore gives $F_w^{\Bcond}=s_BH_B$, $F_w^{\Wcond}=s_WH_W$,
$u_B'=s_BQ_B$, and $u_W'=s_WH_W$.  Since
\begin{equation}
 \frac{\dd}{\dd u}\log\sigm(-u)=-(1-\sigm(-u)),
\end{equation}
differentiating $\Psi$ yields Eq.~\eqref{eq:Psi-prime}.  When both drifts are
positive, monotonicity of the logarithm gives
$\operatorname{sign}(F_w^{\Bcond}-F_w^{\Wcond})=\operatorname{sign}(\Psi)$.

Under $\Psi'\le-\kappa$, integration gives
$\Psi(t)\le\Psi(t_0)-\kappa(t-t_0)$.  Continuity forces a zero by
$t_0+\Psi(t_0)/\kappa\le H$, while strict decrease gives uniqueness.  The
primitive inequalities imply the derivative bound term by term.  \qed

\subsection{Proof of Corollary~\ref{cor:hitting}}
Transversality implies locally before $\tau$ that
$x(t)\le\beta-\kappa(\tau-t)$ and after $\tau$ that
$x(t)\ge\beta+\kappa(t-\tau)$.  Uniform error excludes an $x_N$ crossing before
$\tau-\epsilon_N/\kappa$ and forces one by
$\tau+\epsilon_N/\kappa$.  Separation excludes a spurious earlier crossing.  A
sampling grid adds at most one spacing, and the paired bound follows by the triangle
inequality.  \qed

\subsection{Proof of Theorems~\ref{thm:six-scalar} and
\ref{thm:wide-limit}}
At zero recurrent gain and lag, gradient flow gives
$\dot b_a=g_ac$ and $\dot c=\sum_bg_bb_b$.  Differentiating
$M_a=c\cdot b_a$, $u_{ab}=b_a\cdot b_b$, and $w=c\cdot c$ proves
Eq.~\eqref{eq:six-scalar}.  The mode gradient has one component in the $c$ block
and one in its own $b_a$ block, yielding $G_{ab}=u_{ab}+\delta_{ab}w$.

At initialization, the variances of $M_s,M_w,u_{sw}$ are $1/N$ and those of
$u_{ss}-1,u_{ww}-1,w-1$ are $2/N$.  Coordinatewise Chebyshev bounds and a union
bound sum to $1+1+1+2+2+2=9$, proving
Eq.~\eqref{eq:init-concentration}.

For bounded second cue moments the CE field is locally Lipschitz.  Define the Gram
energy $R=w+\operatorname{tr}(u)$.  Along the six-scalar flow,
\begin{equation}
 R'=4g\cdot M=4\E[X\sigm(-X)],\qquad X=z\cdot M.
\end{equation}
The scalar $x\sigm(-x)$ is bounded above and $R\ge0$, so $R$ cannot blow up on a
finite horizon.  Cauchy--Schwarz bounds all coordinates by $R$, yielding a compact
forward set and a finite local Lipschitz constant on a common tube.  Gronwall's
inequality gives Eq.~\eqref{eq:gronwall}.  Substitute
$\epsilon=\delta e^{-L_HH}$ into Eq.~\eqref{eq:init-concentration} to obtain
Eq.~\eqref{eq:trajectory-concentration}.  The shared-initialization paired result
uses the larger of the two finite-horizon Lipschitz constants.  \qed

\section{Proofs for Counterfactual Drift Correction}
\label{app:cdc-proofs}
\subsection{Instantaneous preservation}
If $a=0$, both strong drifts vanish.  Otherwise,
\begin{equation}
 a\cdot q=a\cdot p-\frac{p\cdot a}{\norm a^2}(a\cdot a)=a\cdot p-p\cdot a=0,
\end{equation}
so $a\cdot v'=a\cdot v+\alpha a\cdot q=a\cdot v$.  This argument is independent
of $\alpha$, hence survives a cap.  \qed

\subsection{Unique minimum-norm correction}
If $\delta\le0$, $d=0$ is feasible and uniquely norm-minimal.  Suppose
$\delta>0$.  Any feasible $d$ obeys $a\cdot d=0$ and $p\cdot d\ge\delta$.
Because $p=q+(p\cdot a/\norm a^2)a$ when $a\ne0$ (and $q=p$ when $a=0$),
$p\cdot d=q\cdot d$.  Cauchy--Schwarz on the subspace orthogonal to $a$ gives
\begin{equation}
 \delta\le q\cdot d\le\norm q\norm d,
 \qquad \norm d\ge\delta/\norm q.
\end{equation}
Equality requires a nonnegative multiple of $q$; the unique target-attaining choice
is $d^*=(\delta/\norm q^2)q$.  A positive deficit is feasible exactly when
$q\ne0$.  \qed

\subsection{Local finite-step deviation}
For an $L$-Lipschitz gradient on both segments, the smoothness remainder satisfies
\begin{equation}
 |m_s(\theta+h)-m_s(\theta)-\nabla m_s(\theta)\cdot h|
 \le\frac L2\norm h^2.
\end{equation}
Apply this at $h=\eta v$ and $h=\eta v'$.  Instantaneous preservation cancels the
linear terms and the triangle inequality yields Eq.~\eqref{eq:cdc-step}.  For
active uncapped CDC,
\begin{equation}
 \norm{v'}\le\norm v+\frac{\delta}{\norm q}\le V+D/q_{\min},
\end{equation}
which gives the explicit bound.  If smoothness is known only on
$B(\theta,r)$, sufficient segment conditions are
$\eta V\le r$ and $\eta(V+D/q_{\min})\le r$.  \qed

\section{Discrete certificate and experiment audit}
For logged values $\Delta_k$, increments telescope exactly:
\begin{equation}
 \Delta_K-\Delta_0=\sum_{k=0}^{K-1}(\Delta_{k+1}-\Delta_k).
\end{equation}
The executable certificate requires one strict block of positive increments followed
by one strict block of negative increments.  The peak-minus-initial value is its
positive area; peak-minus-final is its negative tail area.  It certifies causal
starvation only if the final trajectory is strictly below the initial gap and the
external weak-only learnability flag is true.  This finite-step certificate makes
no assertion between logged points.
